The story

Every signal sits in 3D: time across, harmonic going back (1X, 2X, 3X… each in its own lane), amplitude up. Each sine and cosine inside the signal peels out into its lane, and from the side the lanes become the spectrum.

  • A pure sine splits into one lane: one peak at 1X.
  • A real machine signal (imbalance, misalignment, an impact every turn) splits into peaks everywhere.
  • The reason: the signal repeats every revolution, \(f(t+T)=f(t)\), and only sines and cosines at whole multiples of running speed fit that repeat. The shape decides which lanes fill.

Why the even lanes are exactly zero

The square wave fills only the odd lanes. The video stops to show why. Shift the wave by half a turn and flip it, and it lands exactly on itself:

$$f\!\left(t+\tfrac{T}{2}\right)=-f(t)$$

Give each harmonic the same test. Half a turn is \(n\) half-cycles of the \(n\)th harmonic, so

$$\sin\!\Big(n\omega_0\big(t+\tfrac{T}{2}\big)\Big)=\sin(n\omega_0 t+n\pi)=(-1)^n\sin(n\omega_0 t).$$

Odd harmonics flip with the wave and pass. Even ones come back the right way up, so the flip inverts them. For the whole wave to pass, each even part must equal its own negative:

$$b_n=-b_n\quad\Rightarrow\quad b_n=0\qquad(n\ \text{even}).$$

The video shows it a second way too: each coefficient is an area, \(b_n=\tfrac{2}{T}\int_0^T f(t)\sin(n\omega_0 t)\,dt\). For 2X the green and red areas cancel exactly; for 3X they add up to \(4/(3\pi)\approx0.42\).

The examples

ShapeLanes that fillWhy
SquareOdd only, falling as \(1/n\)Half-wave symmetric; jumps
SawtoothEvery lane, \(1/n\)No flip symmetry
TriangleOdd only, falling as \(1/n^2\)Symmetric; corners instead of jumps
Sine cut on one side1X, the evens, and a DC offsetOne-sided limit breaks the symmetry
Machine signal1X, 2X, and a band around 4X–7XImbalance, misalignment, a ringing impact

Harmonics aren't extra signals. They're the shape of one repeating motion, written as sines and cosines.
