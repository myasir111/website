Plate modes

Derive why sand on a vibrating plate draws a pattern: why grains jump, how two waves make a fixed shape, why only some waves fit, the plate equation, why a pushed mode makes a peak, and where a sensor or a brace should go.

Read the whole lesson as notes · every step, derivation and worked example, on one page

1The puzzle Why does the sand draw a ring?

Sand on a metal plate, shaken at 967 Hz, gathers into a ring and four curved lines. Nobody drew them. Change the frequency a little and the pattern falls apart; change it a lot and a busier one appears.

Why lines? Why only at some frequencies? And why would a sensor standing on one of those lines see almost nothing?

By the end of this lesson we will have answered:

Why does the sand jump at all, and why does it stop on lines?

Why do only some frequencies make a pattern, and why are higher ones busier?

Why does a sensor on a line miss a peak, and why does a brace there do nothing?

Then we solve two examples with every operation shown, and you try one yourself.

7Derivation Why only some waves fit

A wave bouncing between two edges leaves a copy every round trip. Orange: each copy. White: their total. Slide the wavelength: the total only grows when a whole number of half-waves fits, (E2).

A wave on the plate doesn’t bounce once. Every round trip of length 2L2L2L leaves another copy on top of the last, shifted in phase by 2kL2kL2kL; with a little loss rrr per trip the copies add up to

S=∑n=0N−1rne i n 2kLS=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} r^n e^{\,i\,n\,2kL}S=n=0∑N−1​rnein2kL

They all line up only when the shift is a whole number of turns. Any other wavelength and they land out of step and cancel:

2kL=2πm  ⟺  km=mπL2kL=2\pi m\iff k_m=\frac{m\pi}{L}2kL=2πm⟺km​=Lmπ​

That is why a plate only rings at certain frequencies: (13) turns each fitting kkk into its own ω\omegaω. The next step does it exactly for a panel.

9Derivation The free plate: the sand patterns

Free edges have no neat formula; the modes come from a numerical solution (19). These are the film’s plate’s centre-driven modes, the patterns sand draws on a shaker plate.

Step through them: higher frequency, shorter waves, busier lines.

A plate with free edges, bolted at its centre, has no neat formula: its edges must carry no bending moment and no shear, and no product of sines manages that. Its modes are found numerically (here by the Ritz method), as frequency parameters Ω\OmegaΩ:

10Derivation Why a peak

One mode is a mass on a spring with a damper. Push it slowly and it follows; too fast and it can’t keep up; in time and every push adds, until damping takes out what each push puts in, (P3).

11What it means Where the sensor sits

A sensor reads every mode weighted by that mode’s shape where it sits (22). On a nodal line, that mode’s peak is gone (23).

15Summary On one card

Sand runs from motion and stops where W=0W=0W=0. A wave meeting its reflection makes a fixed shape times a clock; the plate equation keeps only shapes that bend back into themselves, each at its own frequency, with bending waves shortening as 1/f1/\sqrt f1/f​.

Every peak in a spectrum is a shape somewhere. A sensor on a nodal line misses it; a brace on one leaves it alone.

    You cut a plate from the same aluminium sheet, twice as long and twice as wide. Where does the film’s 967 Hz ring go? And on a sheet twice as thick, at the original size?

    Show the answer

  3. On site

    Two accelerometers on one duct panel, 30 mm apart. At one mode, one reads 24 dB below the other. What share of the swing does it see, and what do you do?

    Show the answer
    It sees 10−24/20=0.06310^{-24/20}=0.06310−24/20=0.063, about one sixteenth: it sits on or near that mode’s nodal line. Move it off the line, or measure at two or more points away from the middle and the symmetry lines, so no mode can hide.

Chladni’s law. For round plates, acoustics textbooks give an old rule of thumb from Chladni’s own measurements: with mmm nodal diameters and nnn nodal circles, f≈C (m+2n)2f\approx C\,(m+2n)^2f≈C(m+2n)2 The busier the pattern, the higher the note, which is what the lesson’s shortening bending wave predicts.

Why fine powder does the opposite. Very light powder gathers at the places that move most, not on the lines. Faraday showed in 1831 that air currents over the shaking plate carry it there; heavy grains like sand are too heavy for the air to move.
