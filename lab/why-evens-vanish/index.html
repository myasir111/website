Lab · Lesson 3

Why the even harmonics vanish

Shift a wave half a turn, flip it, and see why a symmetric wave has exactly zero 2X, 4X, 6X, and what breaks it.

Read the whole lesson as notes · every step, derivation and worked example, on one page

1Goal Where we are going

A square wave has peaks at 1X, 3X, 5X… and exactly nothing at 2X, 4X, 6X. This lesson proves why, with one idea, symmetry, then tests two signals with numbers.

By the end of this lesson we will have proved, in a few lines, why a square wave has no 2X, 4X, 6X:

f ⁣(t+T2)=−f(t)  ⟺  a0=0 and an=bn=0 for every even nf\!\left(t+\tfrac{T}{2}\right)=-f(t)\iff a_0=0\ \text{and}\ a_n=b_n=0\ \text{for every even } nf(t+2T​)=−f(t)⟺a0​=0 and an​=bn​=0 for every even n
← half-wave symmetry

and what breaks it: a one-sided distortion brings the evens back. Then we test two signals, with numbers.

3The question Start with a square wave

Here is a square wave over two turns of a machine. Below it is its spectrum: peaks at 1X, 3X, 5X, 7X, 9X… and nothing at 2X, 4X, 6X, 8X.

Not small. Exactly zero. Over the next steps we'll see why, using one idea: symmetry.

Over one turn, the square wave is

f(t)={+1,0<t<T2−1,T2<t<Tf(t)=\begin{cases}+1, & 0<t<\tfrac{T}{2}\\[2pt] -1, & \tfrac{T}{2}<t<T\end{cases}f(t)={+1,−1,​0<t<2T​2T​<t<T​
(1)← the square wave

Its spectrum has 1X, 3X, 5X… and 2X = 4X = 6X = 0. Not small: exactly zero. Why?

5Derivation Now flip it

Turn the copy upside down: −f(t+T/2)-f(t+T/2)−f(t+T/2). It lands exactly on the original.

That is the symmetry. Half a turn later, the wave does exactly the opposite of what it is doing now: it pushes down as hard as it pushed up. This is called half-wave symmetry.

Flip the copy: −g(t)=f(t)-g(t)=f(t)−g(t)=f(t), it lands on itself. Substituting (2) in (3):

 f ⁣(t+T2)=−f(t) \boxed{\,f\!\left(t+\tfrac{T}{2}\right)=-f(t)\,}f(t+2T​)=−f(t)​
(4)← half-wave symmetry

6Derivation Give each harmonic the same test

Odd harmonics come back upside down, so the flip puts them back on themselves. Even harmonics come back the right way up, so the flip leaves them inverted.

The square wave passes the test, so every piece of it must pass too. Suppose it contained some 2X, b2sin⁡(2ω0t)b_2\sin(2\omega_0 t)b2​sin(2ω0​t). The test turns that piece into −b2sin⁡(2ω0t)-b_2\sin(2\omega_0 t)−b2​sin(2ω0​t).

9What it means Break the symmetry

Now limit the wave on one side only, like a loose part hitting a stop, or a rub that blocks motion one way. Drag the slider to the left.

Once the limit is one-sided, shift-and-flip no longer gives the wave back, nothing forces the even harmonics to zero, and they appear in the spectrum along with a DC offset.

Now limit the wave on one side only, at a level −L-L−L:

f(t)=max⁡(sin⁡ω0t, −L)f(t)=\max\big(\sin\omega_0 t,\,-L\big)f(t)=max(sinω0​t,−L)
(16)← limited on one side

Shift by half a turn, using (A):

f ⁣(t+T2)=max⁡(−sin⁡ω0t, −L)f\!\left(t+\tfrac{T}{2}\right)=\max(-\sin\omega_0 t,\,-L)f(t+2T​)=max(−sinω0​t,−L)
(17)

Multiply (16) by −1-1−1:

−f(t)=min⁡(−sin⁡ω0t, L)-f(t)=\min(-\sin\omega_0 t,\,L)−f(t)=min(−sinω0​t,L)
(18)

For L<1L<1L<1, (17) and (18) are different, so the symmetry (4) fails:

f ⁣(t+T2)≠−f(t)f\!\left(t+\tfrac{T}{2}\right)\neq-f(t)f(t+2T​)=−f(t)
← symmetry broken

Without (4) we cannot get from (9) to (10), so nothing forces b2,b4,…b_2, b_4,\dotsb2​,b4​,… or a0a_0a0​ to zero: the evens and a DC offset appear.

10What it means What we found

One line of maths explains the empty lanes:

  • If a wave satisfies f(t+T/2)=−f(t)f(t+T/2)=-f(t)f(t+T/2)=−f(t), every even harmonic and the DC term are exactly zero. Square and triangle waves both do.
  • On a machine, a distortion that is the same in both directions adds odd harmonics. A one-sided one (looseness against a stop, a rub, clipping on one side) brings in the even harmonics and a DC offset.

See it in 3D in the harmonics video, or build waves harmonic by harmonic in the next lesson.

In one line:
