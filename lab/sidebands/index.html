Lab · Signals 7

Sidebands

Derive why a slow wobble on a fast tone makes twins, why they are evenly spaced, the ladder a timing wobble makes, why every harmonic gets them and how long a record must be to see them, then use them on a gearbox and a motor.

Read the whole lesson as notes · every step, derivation and worked example, on one page

1The puzzle Why does every peak have twins?

A gearbox spectrum: the gear mesh at 400 Hz and its harmonics, and around every one of them a pair of smaller lines, the same gap apart. Nobody put them there.

Why twins? Why so evenly spaced? What is that 25 Hz gap telling you, and when do twins mean a tooth is in trouble?

By the end of this lesson we will have answered:

Why does a slow wobble on a fast tone make two extra lines?

Why is the gap exactly the wobble’s frequency, and what does a timing wobble do instead?

How long must a record be to see twins at all?

Then we solve two examples with every operation shown, and you try one yourself.

3Derivation A size wobble makes twins

A 400 Hz tone whose size rises and falls once per 25 Hz turn. Multiply it out and use (A): it is three steady tones, the twins each m/2m/2m/2 tall.

4Derivation Why so even

The wobble is two equal arrows turning opposite ways (7). Riding on the carrier’s arrow, their sum only stretches and shrinks it: equal twins, one each side, exactly the wobble’s frequency away.

Drag the wobble frequency: the gap follows it. Then flip one twin, as a timing wobble does (14): the sum stops stretching and swings instead.

The gap. In (5), fcf_cfc​ only sets the middle; the twins sit fmf_mfm​ either side of it, whatever fcf_cfc​ is:

gap=fm\text{gap}=f_mgap=fm​
(6)← the frequency of the wobble, nothing else

The heights. Write the wobble with (B):

1+mcos⁡Ωt=1+m2eiΩt+m2e−iΩt1+m\cos\Omega t=1+\tfrac m2e^{i\Omega t}+\tfrac m2e^{-i\Omega t}1+mcosΩt=1+2m​eiΩt+2m​e−iΩt
(7)

Two arrows of the same length m/2m/2m/2, turning opposite ways. Multiplying by the carrier moves each up by ω\omegaω: one lands at ω+Ω\omega+\Omegaω+Ω, the other at ω−Ω\omega-\Omegaω−Ω. Equal arrows, equal twins. On the board their sum only stretches and shrinks along the carrier: the size wobbles, the angle does not.

In decibels, by (E), each twin sits below the carrier by

5Derivation A timing wobble makes a ladder

Now the teeth come a little early, then a little late. The frequency swings, and (13) gives a ladder of lines, still the wobble’s frequency apart. The lower odd rungs point down: they are flipped.

Drag β\betaβ, the size of the timing wobble. Small β\betaβ: one pair. Large: many.

Now let the timing wobble instead: the tone runs a little ahead, then a little behind, by up to β\betaβ radians:

x(t)=cos⁡(ωt+βsin⁡Ωt)x(t)=\cos(\omega t+\beta\sin\Omega t)x(t)=cos(ωt+βsinΩt)
(10)

The same twins as (4), but one upside down. On the phasor board (step 4), flipping one arrow turns a stretch into a swing.

6Derivation Every harmonic gets them

A real mesh has harmonics, and the wobble rides on all of them. A size wobble gives every harmonic the same twins in proportion (16). A timing wobble moves harmonic kkk by kkk times the angle, so its ladder is wider (17).

A real gear mesh is no pure cosine. It repeats, so it has harmonics (Lessons S1 and S2):

c(t)=∑kakcos⁡kωtc(t)=\sum_ka_k\cos k\omega tc(t)=k∑​ak​coskωt
(15)

A size wobble multiplies all of it. By (4), term by term:

(1+mcos⁡Ωt) akcos⁡kωt=akcos⁡kωt+akm2cos⁡(kω±Ω)t(1+m\cos\Omega t)\,a_k\cos k\omega t=a_k\cos k\omega t+\tfrac{a_km}{2}\cos(k\omega\pm\Omega)t(1+mcosΩt)ak​coskωt=ak​coskωt+2ak​m​cos(kω±Ω)t
(16)← twins around every harmonic (one term each way)

Every harmonic gets twins fmf_mfm​ either side, in the same proportion m/2m/2m/2. A timing wobble shifts time itself, so harmonic kkk is shifted kkk times as much in angle:

akcos⁡(kωt+kβsin⁡Ωt)⇒βk=kβa_k\cos\big(k\omega t+k\beta\sin\Omega t\big)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\beta_k=k\betaak​cos(kωt+kβsinΩt)⇒βk​=kβ
(17)← higher harmonics, wider ladders

7What it means Both at once: uneven twins

An off-centre gear wobbles in size and in timing. The size twins point the same way; one timing twin is flipped. Add them: one side grows, the other shrinks (18).

Make mmm and β\betaβ equal: the lower twin vanishes exactly (20).

An off-centre gear does both: the mesh bites harder and softer (size) and the teeth arrive early and late (timing). Multiply (14) by 1+mcos⁡Ωt1+m\cos\Omega t1+mcosΩt and keep only first-order terms in mmm and β\betaβ:

x(t)≈cos⁡ωt+m+β2cos⁡(ω+Ω)t+m−β2cos⁡(ω−Ω)tx(t)\approx\cos\omega t+\frac{m+\beta}{2}\cos(\omega+\Omega)t+\frac{m-\beta}{2}\cos(\omega-\Omega)tx(t)≈cosωt+2m+β​cos(ω+Ω)t+2m−β​cos(ω−Ω)t
(18)← uneven twins

One side adds, the other cancels. Exactly, multiply the arrows of (7) into (12) and collect the ω−Ω\omega-\Omegaω−Ω terms (every JJJ at β\betaβ):

lower twin=J−1+m2(J0+J−2)=−J1+m2(J0+J2)\text{lower twin}=J_{-1}+\tfrac m2\big(J_0+J_{-2}\big)=-J_1+\tfrac m2\big(J_0+J_2\big)lower twin=J−1​+2m​(J0​+J−2​)=−J1​+2m​(J0​+J2​)
(19)

Bessel functions obey J0(β)+J2(β)=2J1(β)/βJ_0(\beta)+J_2(\beta)=2J_1(\beta)/\betaJ0​(β)+J2​(β)=2J1​(β)/β, so

lower twin=J1(β)(mβ−1)\text{lower twin}=J_1(\beta)\Big(\frac m\beta-1\Big)lower twin=J1​(β)(βm​−1)
(20)← zero when m = β

So twins of different heights mean both wobbles are there (with the two wobbles lined up as in (1) and (10); shifted, the sums move but the twins still differ).

8What it means Seeing them: record at least two turns

The FFT of a short record smears every line into a lobe ±2/T\pm2/T±2/T wide (Lesson S5). Twins 25 Hz apart need the lobes narrower than the gap: a record of more than two turns.

Shorten the record and watch three peaks become one lump.

By (F), every line in a Hann-windowed spectrum is ±2/T\pm2/T±2/T wide at its base. Twins fmf_mfm​ apart stand out as separate peaks only when each one’s lobe is narrower than the gap:

2T<fm  ⟺  T>2fm\frac2T<f_m\iff T>\frac{2}{f_m}T2​<fm​⟺T>fm​2​
(21)

In turns of the wobble, Tfm>2Tf_m>2Tfm​>2: at least two turns. Shorter, and leakage blurs the twins into the carrier, one lump.

fm=25 Hz:T>225=0.08 sf_m=25\ \text{Hz}:\quad T>\frac{2}{25}=0.08\ \text{s}fm​=25 Hz:T>252​=0.08 s
(22)← the board finds three peaks from about 2.5 turns

12Summary On one card

Sidebands are a slow wobble written as sines and cosines. A size wobble gives a pair, a timing wobble a ladder; both sit the wobble’s frequency apart, around every harmonic. Uneven twins mean both. To see them, record more than two turns.

Small and steady is normal. Growing or multiplying is a reason to look, and the gap says where.

Practice

Three problems: a warm-up, a stretch and one from site. Try each before you open the answer.

  1. Warm-up

    A gear mesh at 400 Hz has twins at 375 and 425 Hz. The pinion turns at 1,500 rpm. What is doing the wobbling?

    Show the answer
    The gap is 425−400=25425-400 = 25425−400=25 Hz, and 1500/60=251500/60 = 251500/60=25 Hz. The twins are spaced at the pinion’s 1X, so the pinion is the one whose mesh force wobbles once per turn.

From the textbooks

How wide a family of sidebands gets

Communication textbooks give Carson’s rule for a timing wobble of index β\betaβ at fmf_mfm​: almost all the power (about 98% or more) sits within ±(β+1)\pm(\beta+1)±(β+1) sidebands, a total width of 2(β+1)fm2(\beta+1)f_m2(β+1)fm​. So a wide family of sidebands points to a large timing wobble; a wobble in size alone gives one pair.
