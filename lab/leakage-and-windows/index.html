Lab · Lesson 5

Leakage and windows

Derive why a tone between two FFT bins leaks into all of them, what a Hann window does about it and the ×2 correction, then work one pump signal both ways on a calculator.

Read the whole lesson as notes · every step, derivation and worked example, on one page

1Goal Where we are going

One clean tone should give one clean line in a spectrum. Often it gives a blurred hill instead. This lesson derives exactly why, and what a window does about it, then works one pump signal through both ways.

By the end of this lesson we will have derived why a tone that falls between two FFT bins leaks into all of them, and by how much:

2 ∣Xk∣≈A ∣sin⁡π(f−k)π (f−k)∣2\,|X_k|\approx A\,\left|\frac{\sin\pi(f-k)}{\pi\,(f-k)}\right|2∣Xk​∣≈A​π(f−k)sinπ(f−k)​​
← no window

what a Hann window does to the same tone (after the ×2 correction every analyser applies):

readingk≈A ∣sin⁡π(f−k)∣π ∣f−k∣  ∣(f−k)2−1∣\text{reading}_k\approx A\,\frac{|\sin\pi(f-k)|}{\pi\,|f-k|\;\big|(f-k)^2-1\big|}readingk​≈Aπ∣f−k∣​(f−k)2−1​∣sinπ(f−k)∣​

Here fff is the number of cycles of the tone in the record, and kkk is the bin. Then we solve one pump signal twice on a calculator, with and without the window.

3Derivation A tone into the DFT

Write the tone as two arrows and put one of them into the DFT. Out comes a geometric series: NNN arrows, each turned by the same small step.

The step depends on f−kf-kf−k: how far the tone is from the bin you are computing.

4Derivation A whole number of cycles

If the tone fits the record exactly, the arrows either all line up (in its own bin) or close into a loop (in every other bin).

6What it means The jump at the seam

The FFT treats the record as if it repeated. If the tone doesn’t fit, the end of one copy doesn’t meet the start of the next: there is a jump at every seam, and a jump needs many frequencies.

Slide fff, and switch the window on to see the seams close.

Why does a tone that doesn’t fit behave so differently? The DFT treats the record as one period of a repeating signal (Lesson 4). Compare the two ends of the record in (1), with φ=0\varphi=0φ=0:

x0=A,xN=Acos⁡(2πf)x_0=A,\qquad x_N=A\cos(2\pi f)x0​=A,xN​=Acos(2πf)
(13)

If fff is a whole number, cos⁡(2πf)=1\cos(2\pi f)=1cos(2πf)=1 and the ends meet. Otherwise there is a jump at every seam. A jump is a sharp edge, and by Lesson 2, (14), sharp edges need harmonics that fall off only like 1/n1/n1/n. That is the same slow 1/∣f−k∣1/|f-k|1/∣f−k∣ as in (11).

7Derivation The Hann window is three arrows

A Hann window is a constant minus a cosine, and a cosine is two arrows. Multiplying a signal by an arrow shifts its whole spectrum by one bin.

So windowing never needs a new FFT: each new bin is half the old bin, minus a quarter of each neighbour.

The fix is to fade the record in and out so the ends meet at zero. The Hann window is

wn=12(1−cos⁡2πnN)w_n=\tfrac12\Big(1-\cos\frac{2\pi n}{N}\Big)wn​=21​(1−cosN2πn​)
(14)← 0 at both ends, 1 in the middle

By (B), the cosine is two arrows, so the window is three terms:

wn=12−14 e i2πn/N−14 e−i2πn/Nw_n=\tfrac12-\tfrac14\,e^{\,i2\pi n/N}-\tfrac14\,e^{-i2\pi n/N}wn​=21​−41​ei2πn/N−41​e−i2πn/N
(15)

What does multiplying a signal by e i2πn/Ne^{\,i2\pi n/N}ei2πn/N do to its DFT? Put it into (A):

1N∑nxn e i2πn/N e−i2πkn/N=1N∑nxn e−i2π(k−1)n/N=Xk−1\frac{1}{N}\sum_n x_n\,e^{\,i2\pi n/N}\,e^{-i2\pi kn/N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_n x_n\,e^{-i2\pi(k-1)n/N}=X_{k-1}N1​n∑​xn​ei2πn/Ne−i2πkn/N=N1​n∑​xn​e−i2π(k−1)n/N=Xk−1​
(16)← it shifts everything up one bin

In the same way e−i2πn/Ne^{-i2\pi n/N}e−i2πn/N gives Xk+1X_{k+1}Xk+1​. So the DFT of the windowed record wnxnw_nx_nwn​xn​, call it YkY_kYk​, is by (15) and (16)

(17)← each bin minus half the average of its neighbours

8Derivation A tone on a bin, through the window

Even a tone that fits perfectly is changed by the window: its bin reads half, and its two neighbours each pick up a quarter. Analysers multiply by 2 to put the peak right.

The board shows the corrected spectrum: the right peak, with two neighbours at half height.

Try (17) on a tone that fits the record, f=mf=mf=m. By (5) and (6), Xm=A2eiφX_m=\tfrac{A}{2}e^{i\varphi}Xm​=2A​eiφ and every other Xk=0X_k=0Xk​=0:

(19)← the window removed half the signal

So an analyser multiplies a Hann-windowed spectrum by 2, the amplitude correction. (2 is one over the average height of the window, which is 12\tfrac1221​ by (14).) After correction:

Second, the tone now occupies three bins, not one. That is the price of a window: a wider peak.

9Derivation Why the far leak collapses

Without a window, neighbouring bins of a leaking tone are nearly equal. The window subtracts half the average of the neighbours from each bin, so far from the tone almost everything cancels.

Switch between the two on the board. Open circles are the formulas.
