AIV sound power level

High-pressure gas letdown through a valve, PSV or orifice radiates intense noise inside the downstream pipe. The sound power level is step one in screening for acoustic-induced vibration fatigue.

Sound power level
—dB

Sensitivity: change in PWL if you double the flow, or halve the pressure drop.

The formula

PWL = 10 · log10[ W² · (ΔP / P1)3.6 · (T / Mw)1.2 ] + 126.1 + K

W in kg/s, T in kelvin, ΔP = P1 − P2; K is 0 for subsonic and 6 dB for sonic (choked) flow. This is the Carucci–Mueller correlation, built from field data on piping failures downstream of pressure-reducing devices. It's the basis of the sound power step in the Energy Institute AIV screening method.

Notice how strongly flow drives it: PWL rises with 20·log(W), so doubling the flow adds 6 dB. Splitting a large letdown across stages, or across several smaller devices, is one of the main design levers.

What this does not do: the likelihood-of-failure assessment. That also depends on the downstream pipe diameter and wall thickness, the distance to the nearest branch or discontinuity, branch connection types and sound attenuation along the line, all defined in the Energy Institute guidelines. Use this to see quickly whether a source is quiet or loud, and the full method for the assessment.
