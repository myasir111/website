Miles' equation

A quick estimate of how hard a component responds to broadband random vibration, as long as the input spectrum is roughly flat around its natural frequency.

Q = 1/(2ζ). Q = 10 (ζ = 5%) is a common default when damping is unknown.

The formulas

Grms = √( (π/2) · fn · Q · PSD(fn) )
zrms ≈ Grms · g / (2πfn)²

The (π/2)·fn/Q term is the effective noise bandwidth of the resonance, so the formula is just "input density × bandwidth × amplification²". Peaks in a Gaussian random response are commonly taken at 3σ (3 × RMS), which covers 99.7% of the time.

Assumes a single dominant mode and a flat input PSD around fn. If the PSD slopes steeply near the resonance, or several modes contribute, integrate the full response PSD instead.
