Start here

If you only read a handful of things, make it these.

  • Energy Institute (2008). Guidelines for the avoidance of vibration induced fatigue failure in process pipework, 2nd edition Guideline The industry's go-to screening method for FIV, AIV, pulsation and small-bore connections. If you assess piping vibration, you'll live in this.
  • Randall, R. B. (2011). Vibration-based condition monitoring: industrial, aerospace and automotive applications. Wiley Book The best single book on the signal processing behind machine diagnostics: envelope analysis, cepstrum, order tracking, bearing and gear faults.
  • Den Hartog, J. P. Mechanical vibrations. Dover Book Old, cheap and still one of the clearest books on the subject. Physical intuition first, maths second.
  • Blevins, R. D. (1979, reprinted 2001). Formulas for natural frequency and mode shape. Krieger Book The handbook for hand calculations: beam, plate, shell and acoustic natural frequencies for almost every boundary condition. Behind several of the tools on this site.
  • 3Blue1Brown: But what is the Fourier Transform? A visual introduction Video The "winding a signal around a circle" intuition. The best 20 minutes you can spend on the Fourier transform.

Piping vibration: FIV, AIV and pulsation

  • Wachel, J. C., Morton, S. J. and Atkins, K. E. (1990). Piping vibration analysis. Proceedings of the 19th Turbomachinery Symposium, Texas A&M University, 119-134 Paper Field-proven practice: span frequency corrections, the velocity-to-stress relationship and how to screen measured piping vibration.
  • Blevins, R. D. Flow-induced vibration, 2nd edition. Krieger Book The classic text on vortex shedding, galloping and flow-excited structures. Heavy, but it's where the physics behind the screening rules lives.
  • Flow-excited acoustic resonance in ducts with closed side branches. Proceedings of the 9th International Conference on Flow-Induced Vibration (FIV 2008), paper 291 Paper Experiments showing the Strouhal range (about 0.27 to 0.55) over which closed side branches lock onto their acoustic modes.
  • Carucci, V. A. and Mueller, R. T. (1982). Acoustically induced piping vibration in high capacity pressure reducing systems. ASME paper 82-WA/PVP-8 Paper Where the AIV sound power equation comes from, built on failure data from real let-down systems.
  • Eisinger, F. L. (1997). Designing piping systems against acoustically induced structural fatigue. Journal of Pressure Vessel Technology 119(3), 379-383 Paper Turns the Carucci–Mueller data into a design limit on sound power as a function of pipe D/t.
  • ASME OM Part 3. Requirements for preoperational and initial start-up vibration testing of piping systems Standard Velocity-based screening of piping vibration and the relation between vibration velocity and stress for beam-type modes.
  • API Standard 618, 5th edition. Reciprocating compressors for petroleum, chemical and gas industry services Standard Pulsation and mechanical response design for reciprocating compressor systems, including the common 20% separation margin.
  • US Patent 7,430,914 B2 (2008) Patent A publicly available document that reproduces the EI main-line screening line equations. Handy for checking your own implementation.
  • ASME PTC 19.3 TW-2016. Thermowells Standard The dedicated method for thermowell vortex-shedding and stress checks.

Worked examples to check your calculations

Published worked examples I've used to verify FIV and AIV calculations against hand results.

  • Bachoo, R. (2021). Flow induced vibrations of oil and gas piping systems. The West Indian Journal of Engineering 43(2), 54-68 Paper A complete EI FIV likelihood-of-failure example on a 3-inch line with medium supports.
  • AFT Fathom 13 documentation. Example: flow induced vibration calculations Worked example An EI FIV example on an 18-inch water line with stiff supports, step by step.
  • Gas Processing & LNG (2020). Calculate likelihood of piping failure from high-frequency acoustic vibration Article Worked AIV examples, including sound power and attenuation along the line.
  • Best practice to analyze vibration in piping systems (2025). International Journal of Innovative Science and Research Technology, IJISRT25OCT1557 Paper A recent overview of piping vibration assessment practice, including support classification for FIV screening.

Machinery diagnostics & condition monitoring

  • ISO 20816-3:2022. Mechanical vibration: measurement and evaluation of machine vibration. Part 3: industrial machinery Standard Evaluation zones for industrial machines measured on non-rotating parts. Replaces ISO 10816-3:2009, with the same zone boundaries for groups 1 and 2.
  • ISO 18436-2. Condition monitoring and diagnostics of machines: requirements for qualification and assessment of personnel. Part 2: vibration condition monitoring and diagnostics Standard The competence requirements behind vibration analyst certification (Categories I–IV). A useful map of what a complete analyst should know.

Signal processing

  • Harris, F. J. (1978). On the use of windows for harmonic analysis with the discrete Fourier transform. Proceedings of the IEEE 66(1), 51-83 Paper The reference on window functions: noise bandwidth, scalloping loss and leakage for every common window.
  • Gade, S. and Herlufsen, H. (1987). Use of weighting functions in DFT/FFT analysis, parts I and II. Brüel & Kjær Technical Review 3 and 4 Technical review Practical window corrections for tones versus broadband signals: why amplitude and energy corrections differ.
  • Grandke, T. (1983). Interpolation algorithms for discrete Fourier transforms of weighted signals. IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement 32(2), 350-355 Paper How to estimate a peak's true frequency between FFT bins.
  • Welch, P. D. (1967). The use of fast Fourier transform for the estimation of power spectra: a method based on time averaging over short, modified periodograms. IEEE Transactions on Audio and Electroacoustics 15(2), 70-73 Paper The overlapped, windowed, averaged spectrum that every data collector (and the FFT analyser here) uses.
  • MathWorks. flattopwin: flat top weighted window (documentation) Software docs The five-term flat-top window coefficients used by the FFT analyser.
  • Jack Schaedler: Seeing circles, sines and signals Interactive An interactive primer on sampling, aliasing and the DFT. A big inspiration for the explainers on this site.
  • Bartosz Ciechanowski Interactive essays Not about vibration, but the gold standard for teaching physical systems with interactive figures.

Structural dynamics & finite elements

  • Rao, S. S. Mechanical vibrations. Pearson Book The standard university textbook: thorough, well-structured, lots of worked examples.
  • Leissa, A. W. (1969). Vibration of plates. NASA SP-160 Free report The definitive survey of plate vibration for every shape and edge condition, free from NASA.
  • Leissa, A. W. (1973). Vibration of shells. NASA SP-288 Free report The definitive survey of shell vibration, free from NASA. Pipe shell modes matter for AIV and high-frequency vibration.
  • Soedel, W. (2004). Vibrations of shells and plates, 3rd edition. Marcel Dekker Book Shell and plate modes, including the added mass of contents.
  • Przemieniecki, J. S. (1968). Theory of matrix structural analysis. McGraw-Hill Book Classic derivations of frame element stiffness and mass matrices, including shear-deformable (Timoshenko) beams.
  • Cowper, G. R. (1966). The shear coefficient in Timoshenko beam theory. Journal of Applied Mechanics 33(2), 335-340 Paper Shear coefficients for common sections, including hollow circular pipe.
  • Bathe, K.-J. (2014). Finite element procedures, 2nd edition Book The rigorous FE reference: element formulations, solvers and eigenvalue methods.
  • Cuthill, E. and McKee, J. (1969). Reducing the bandwidth of sparse symmetric matrices. Proceedings of the 24th ACM National Conference, 157-172 Paper Node renumbering that makes FE solvers fast. Useful if you write your own solver.
  • Miles, J. W. (1954). On structural fatigue under random loading. Journal of the Aeronautical Sciences 21(11), 753-762 Paper The origin of Miles' equation for the RMS response of a resonator to random excitation.

Rotordynamics & bearings

  • Friswell, M. I., Penny, J. E. T., Garvey, S. D. and Lees, A. W. (2010). Dynamics of rotating machines. Cambridge University Press Book The modern rotordynamics textbook: rotor elements, gyroscopics, bearings, unbalance response and stability, with MATLAB examples.
  • Childs, D. (1993). Turbomachinery rotordynamics: phenomena, modeling and analysis. Wiley Book Bearing and seal coefficients, cross-coupled stiffness and stability in depth.
  • API Recommended Practice 684, 2nd edition. Rotordynamic tutorial Recommended practice Amplification factor, separation margins, stability screening and torsional analysis practice for API machines.
  • API Standard 617, 9th edition (2022). Axial and centrifugal compressors and expander-compressors Standard Lateral analysis and stability acceptance criteria for compressors.
  • API Standard 610, 12th edition (2021). Centrifugal pumps for petroleum, petrochemical and natural gas industries Standard Pump lateral analysis (Annex I): damping, separation margins and clearance limits.
  • Alford, J. S. (1965). Protecting turbomachinery from self-excited rotor whirl. Journal of Engineering for Power 87(4), 333-344 Paper The origin of aerodynamic cross-coupling at impellers, still used (in modified form) in API 684.
  • Ocvirk, F. W. (1952). Short-bearing approximation for full journal bearings. NACA Technical Note 2808 Free report The short-bearing theory behind quick journal bearing calculations.
  • ASTM D341. Viscosity-temperature equations and charts for liquid petroleum products Standard How lubricant viscosity changes with temperature: needed for bearing calculations.
  • ISO 3448:1992. Industrial liquid lubricants: ISO viscosity classification Standard The ISO VG grades.

Piping codes & components

  • ASME B31.3 Process piping Code The process piping code. Its Appendix D flexibility factors moved to ASME B31J in later editions.
  • ASME B31J-2017. Stress intensification factors and flexibility factors for metallic piping components Code The current home of SIFs and k-factors for elbows, tees and branches.
  • ASME B36.10M. Welded and seamless wrought steel pipe Code Pipe outside diameters and wall thicknesses by schedule.
  • ASME B16.9. Factory-made wrought buttwelding fittings Code Elbow, tee and reducer dimensions.
  • Hexagon. CAESAR II user guide and technical reference Software docs How the industry-standard pipe stress program models restraints, hangers, rigid elements and bends.

Fatigue

  • DNV-RP-C203. Fatigue design of offshore steel structures Recommended practice S-N curves, thickness effects and mean-stress treatment. Free to download from DNV.
  • BS 7608:2014+A1:2015. Guide to fatigue design and assessment of steel products Standard The UK weld class S-N curves.
  • Hobbacher, A. F. (2016). Recommendations for fatigue design of welded joints and components, 2nd edition. IIW / Springer Book The IIW FAT classes for welded details.
  • ASTM E1049-85 (2017). Standard practices for cycle counting in fatigue analysis Standard The definition of rainflow counting.
  • Miner, M. A. (1945). Cumulative damage in fatigue. Journal of Applied Mechanics 12, A159-A164 Paper Linear damage summation: where Miner's rule comes from.
  • Wirsching, P. H. and Light, M. C. (1980). Fatigue under wide band random stresses. Journal of the Structural Division, ASCE 106(7), 1593-1607 Paper Correcting narrowband fatigue damage for real, wide-band stress spectra.
  • Dirlik, T. (1985). Application of computers in fatigue analysis. PhD thesis, University of Warwick Thesis The most widely used method for fatigue damage straight from a stress PSD.
  • Benasciutti, D. and Tovo, R. (2005). Spectral methods for lifetime prediction under wide-band stationary random processes. International Journal of Fatigue 27(8), 867-877 Paper A more recent and often more accurate spectral fatigue method.
  • Zhao, W. and Baker, M. J. (1992). On the probability density function of rainflow stress range for stationary Gaussian processes. International Journal of Fatigue 14(2), 121-135 Paper Another spectral method for rainflow range distribution.
  • Zorman, A., Slavič, J. and Boltežar, M. (2023). Vibration fatigue by spectral methods: a review with open-source support. Mechanical Systems and Signal Processing 190, 110149 Paper The best single overview of spectral fatigue methods, with an open-source implementation (FLife).
  • Steinberg, D. S. (2000). Vibration analysis for electronic equipment, 3rd edition. Wiley Book The practical three-band (1σ, 2σ, 3σ) fatigue method and plenty of random vibration rules of thumb.

Open-source software & free libraries

  • ROSS: Rotordynamic Open Source Software (Python). Timbo, R. et al. (2020), Journal of Open Source Software 5(48), 2120 Software A well-documented rotordynamics library. Excellent for checking your own rotor models.
  • FLife: vibration fatigue library (Python), University of Ljubljana Software Reference implementations of the spectral fatigue methods above.
  • Tom Irvine: Vibrationdata Tutorials & software Decades of free tutorials, papers and programs on shock, random vibration, SRS, PSD and fatigue. The inspiration for the tools on this site.
