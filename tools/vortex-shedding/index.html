Vortex shedding frequency

Flow past a cylinder (a thermowell, a pipe in wind or current, a tube in a bundle) sheds vortices at a predictable frequency. If that lands on a natural frequency, it can lock in and drive large vibration.

St ≈ 0.2 for a circular cylinder over a wide subcritical Reynolds range (roughly 300 to 2×10⁵).

The formulas

fs = St · V / D    Re = ρVD / μ    Vr = V / (fn·D)

Vortices shed alternately from each side, so the lift force (cross-flow) oscillates at fs while the drag force (in-line) oscillates at about 2·fs. In-line vibration can therefore start at roughly half the velocity needed for cross-flow lock-in, which matters for thermowells in liquid service.

This is a screening check. Lock-in depends on mass ratio, damping and the actual geometry, and thermowells have their own dedicated method (ASME PTC 19.3 TW). Use those for design.

Method & sources

What it calculates

  • Strouhal relation fs = St·V/D for cross-flow (lift) shedding; in-line (drag) shedding at 2·fs.
  • Reynolds number Re = ρVD/μ to check the flow regime for the chosen St; reduced velocity Vr = V/(fn·D).

Assumptions & limits

  • St ≈ 0.2 holds for circular cylinders over the subcritical range; it changes near the drag crisis (Re around 2×10⁵ and above).
  • Lock-in depends on mass ratio and damping (Scruton number), which are not included. Thermowells must be assessed to ASME PTC 19.3 TW.

Verified: 5 m/s past a 25 mm cylinder in water gives fs = 40 Hz and Re = 1.25×10⁵, matching Python.
